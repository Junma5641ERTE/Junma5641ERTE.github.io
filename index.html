About Me

I am a doctoral researcher at Université de Technologie de Compiègne (UTC), LMAC Laboratory, supervised by Prof. Faker Ben Belgacem and Prof. Faten Jelassi. My research integrates topology optimization and PDE analysis with AI-driven modeling for thermal energy systems, including phase change materials (PCM) and Kapitza interface laws.

I have recently been following developments in AI for Mathematics (AI4Math), particularly how AI can assist mathematical reasoning and research.

Previously, I obtained my Master's degree in Computational Mathematics from Northwestern Polytechnical University (Double First-Class/985 University), under the supervision of Academician Weihong Zhang, Prof. Manyu Xiao, and Prof. Piotr Breitkopf. I received my Bachelor's degree in Mathematics and Applied Mathematics from the same institution.

PhD Research Progress

Selected research activities and working directions from my doctoral study at UTC-LMAC.

Kapitza interface laws
Nonlinear contact resistance and bifurcation analysis

Study of steady heat conduction across composite-domain interfaces with temperature-dependent Kapitza resistance, including existence, non-uniqueness, bifurcation, and hysteresis phenomena.

Numerical analysis
Galerkin and FEM approximation of interface problems

Finite element analysis for linear and nonlinear Kapitza interface models, with implementation in FreeFEM and ongoing work on discrete maximum-principle and L-infinity estimates.

AI for PDEs
PINN-based multi-solution discovery

Development of a probabilistic framework where PINNs act as randomized seed generators, followed by Newton/FEM refinement and certification of multiple nonlinear PDE solutions.

Research presentation
From Kapitza bifurcations to PINN-based discovery

May 2026 research presentation connecting Kapitza bifurcation theory, probabilistic completeness for multi-solution discovery, and XPINN experiments. Slides
