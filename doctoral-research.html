Nonlinear Kapitza contact: research begun in Year 1

A central first-year theme was steady conduction in layered media with nonlinear Kapitza contact resistance. Imperfect contact permits a temperature jump across the interface and can produce non-unique steady states. In the collaborative study, one-dimensional models with power-law contact conductance were used to investigate fixed points, parameter sensitivity, and bifurcations of Picard iteration. The stability discussed here concerns the iteration map, not the physical stability of a time-dependent heat equation.

I carried out the numerical experiments and validation for this work. The theoretical proofs were developed by Eya Bejaoui, Faker Ben Belgacem, and Faten Jelassi. This division of contributions also applies to our subsequent HAL preprint on semilinear elliptic problems.

Research year and publication year. The first-year study led to Steady conduction problems with non-linear Kapitza contact resistance: Existence and non-uniqueness, by E. Bejaoui, F. Ben Belgacem, F. Jelassi, and J. Ma. The manuscript was first submitted on 9 July 2025 and published online on 12 April 2026 in Mathematics and Computers in Simulation, 248: 311–324. Its publication is a Year 2 milestone for research begun in Year 1.

Thermal modeling and computation

The work began with steady and transient heat-conduction models for hybrid thermal-energy-storage systems. Analytical derivations and numerical experiments were used together to understand temperature fields, heat-flux evolution, and the role of material parameters. One- and two-dimensional FreeFEM++ simulations provided a computational basis for the later finite-element and optimization studies.

Radiative effects and other nonlinear thermal mechanisms motivated a gradual shift from classical conduction models toward nonlinear partial differential equations. This stage clarified how modeling assumptions, interface behavior, and numerical implementation interact in composite thermal systems.

Topology optimization and research formation

In parallel, I studied topological derivatives and implemented an inverse-design procedure for two-dimensional transient heat conduction. This connected thermal sensitivity analysis with the placement of conductive material. Doctoral schools, FreeFEM meetings, and research workshops in optimization and scientific computing supported the formation of a broader methodological framework.

The Year 1 report records these research activities before the journal publication. The homepage also uses a later illustration from my 21 July 2026 research note on an exponential contact law; that figure illustrates the broader fixed-point theme and is not a figure from the MATCOM article.

Finite elements for Kapitza interface problems

I developed FreeFEM and MATLAB tools for linear and nonlinear thermal contact laws and began consolidating the linear Kapitza finite-element framework: weak formulation, separate temperature traces, interface terms, and discretization. These activities connected the continuous model to the numerical schemes and prepared further analysis of nonlinear solvability and branch behavior.

Nonlinear branches and robust computation

Numerical experiments showed that standard Newton iteration and direct parameter continuation can become sensitive when several solution branches or turning points are present. Pseudo-arclength continuation was therefore explored as a more robust way to follow branches through folds. The emphasis was not only on producing solutions, but also on understanding branch selection, dependence on initial guesses, and the distinction between computational observations and mathematical conclusions.

The homepage's S-curve comes from my 23 July 2026 research note on a source-controlled model with exponential contact conductance. It is a later model investigation; attraction and repulsion in that figure refer specifically to Picard iteration.

Topological derivatives and thermal optimization

Building on the first-year foundations, I implemented and numerically checked topological-derivative criteria for material modification. These experiments linked thermal sensitivity analysis to the placement of conductive inclusions and provided a basis for further optimization studies.

Scientific machine learning as a complementary tool

I implemented preliminary XPINN solvers for steady and time-dependent heat conduction with spatially varying conductivity. These domain-decomposition experiments form a starting point for interface learning. The homepage's Kapitza-aware architecture illustrates a proposed extension; full nonlinear Kapitza and phase-change validation remain research directions. PINN and XPINN methods are being explored as complementary tools for repeated simulations and optimization.

03
Year 3 · Ongoing

Nonlinear Kapitza interfaces in one and two dimensions

The current stage connects structural insight from one-dimensional models with the geometric and numerical questions arising in two dimensions.

One-dimensional model analysis

One-dimensional configurations provide a controlled setting in which interface temperatures, contact laws, and the structure of the solution set can be separated and examined clearly. The present work uses these models to organize questions of existence, boundedness, multiplicity, and stability without obscuring them behind geometric complexity.

Two-dimensional finite-element questions

In two dimensions, the same physical mechanism interacts with interface geometry, finite-element spaces, mesh structure, and boundary conditions. The ongoing work studies how these ingredients affect reliable approximation and the control of discrete solutions. Particular attention is given to the assumptions under which analytical estimates and computational observations remain valid.

Research positioning

The objective is to build a coherent bridge between model-level understanding and dependable computation in composite domains. The work distinguishes established results from current investigations and uses numerical experiments to guide, rather than replace, mathematical analysis. This public description intentionally remains high level while the detailed mathematical scope continues to be refined.

Current focus. One-dimensional solution structure, two-dimensional finite-element stability, and reliable computation for nonlinear Kapitza interface models.
